SINGAPORE — Nowhere else on earth does the simple privilege of owning an engine require a sovereign lottery ticket priced equivalent to an executive condominium down payment. Yet twice every month, thousands of prospective car buyers and automotive fleet syndicates sit in front of bidding terminals across Singapore, watching a dynamic Dutch auction dictate the cost of mobility.

Introduced in May 1990 under the Vehicle Quota System (VQS), the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) represents a masterclass in macroeconomic demand-side rationing. The premise is disarmingly simple: rather than permitting gridlock to choke an island territory spanning barely 735 square kilometers, the state caps vehicle fleet growth to zero percent per annum. To own a car for 10 years, one must purchase one of a finite number of certificates auctioned fortnightly.

The Great Post-Pandemic Spike & The S$150,000 Shock

Between 2020 and late 2023, a confluence of supply contractions and post-pandemic liquidity drove premiums into uncharted territory. Quotas, which are governed by a 10-year deregistrations replacement formula, plunged into a cyclical trough just as electric vehicle (EV) luxury brands and private-hire vehicle fleets unleashed unprecedented demand.

“When a certificate of entitlement costs more than the automobile itself, the market is no longer pricing sheet metal and rubber — it is pricing road space as ultra-scarce real estate.”

By October 2023, Category B (larger and luxury sedans) breached an all-time record of S$150,001, while Category E (the Open category favored by luxury conglomerates) soared to S$158,004. Even Category A — originally established to protect mass-market family runabouts — crossed S$100,000, creating what transport economists dubbed the “Scissor Squeeze.”

The Mechanics of Equilibrium: Quota vs. Bids Received

To understand why premiums escalate so violently, one must examine the Bid-to-Quota Ratio. Unlike typical commodity markets where supply expands when prices surge, Singapore's vehicle quota is strictly exogenous — determined months in advance by the Land Transport Authority (LTA) based on historical de-registrations.

When supply shrinks by 20%, bids do not retreat proportionally. Instead, bidding cartels, corporate fleets, and wealthy households bid aggressively to secure guaranteed certificates, causing non-linear exponential price spikes. In peak months, Category E frequently sees bid-to-quota ratios topping 1.8x, triggering blind auctions where dealers push bids upward to avoid showroom forfeits.

The 2023 “Cut-and-Fill” Supply Injection

By November 2023, public anxiety over record-breaking COE prices prompted the Ministry of Transport (MOT) to take unprecedented structural action. Rather than waiting for the natural 10-year deregistrations cycle to recover in 2025–2026, the government introduced the “cut-and-fill” strategy.

By borrowing and bringing forward guaranteed quotas from the projected 2026–2028 peak deregistrations, policymakers injected thousands of additional quotas into Category A, B, and C over multiple quarters. The immediate market response was profound: Category B plunged from S$150,001 in October 2023 to S$85,010 in January 2024 — an instantaneous S$65,000 relief valve before speculative bidding reasserted upward pressure.

Strategic Takeaways & The Future of Mobility

As Singapore approaches the late 2020s, the Vehicle Quota System remains the cornerstone of its sustainable urban planning. Yet the empirical data reveals three key strategic lessons for market participants:

1. Category Convergence: Category A has ceased to be a pure “budget” bracket. As premium EV manufacturers tune their drivetrains to sit just under the 110kW threshold, Category A and B premiums have increasingly tracked each other within a tight 5–8% delta.

2. Counter-Cyclical Front-Loading: The success of the 2023 cut-and-fill policy proved that smoothing the 10-year supply cycle dampens speculative panic, although fundamental scarcity ensures premiums remain structurally high.

3. Fleet Dominance: With corporate and ride-hailing fleets bidding via Category E and converting them to car hire registrations, private individual buyers will face persistent institutional competition during economic expansions.
